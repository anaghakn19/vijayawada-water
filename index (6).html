1. Overview

Question. How much of Vijayawada is covered by seasonal surface water each year, and how variable is this area over 2001–2021?

Boundary. Vijayawada municipal boundary from DataMeet Municipal Spatial Data (github.com/datameet/Municipal_Spatial_Data).

2. Method

  1. The boundary was read with GeoPandas, converted to WGS84, stripped of z-coordinates, dissolved to one polygon and converted to an Earth Engine FeatureCollection.
  2. The 21 annual waterClass images (2001–2021) were loaded and clipped to the boundary, using the same 30 m scale for every year.
  3. Seasonal water was extracted as waterClass == 2. Annual area (km²) was obtained by summing ee.Image.pixelArea() over the seasonal pixels inside the boundary; permanent, not-water and no-data areas were computed the same way.
  4. Variability and trend were summarised with mean, standard deviation, coefficient of variation, Theil–Sen slope and the Mann–Kendall (Kendall τ) test; the first five years (2001–2005) were compared with the last five (2017–2021).
  5. Two thematic maps were made: the number of years each pixel was seasonal water, and the change in that frequency between 2001–2005 and 2017–2021.
  6. An animated GIF of the annual water class was produced from the 2001–2021 image collection.

Main spatial pattern

The frequency map (Section 4.2) shows where seasonal water is concentrated, and the selected-year maps (Section 4.1) show how its extent changes between years. Seasonal water is expected mainly along the Krishna river channel and its banks near the Prakasam Barrage and in low-lying areas, where flooding and seasonal inundation are most likely. Pixels with high frequency values are persistently seasonal, while pixels with values of 1–3 are occasional and mostly reflect individual wet years. The change map (Section 4.3) separates the parts of the city where seasonal water became more frequent from those where it became less frequent.

Possible explanations

Year-to-year changes in seasonal water in a river city are plausibly linked to monsoon rainfall, river discharge, releases from the barrage and upstream reservoirs, flood events, and land-use change near the river. These are hypotheses: the dataset records where and for how long water was present, not why, so no causal claim is made here without independent rainfall or flood records.

8. Limitations

  • Resolution. 30 m pixels miss small ponds and narrow channels, and each year gets only one class, so the months of water presence are unknown.
  • Data availability. Cloud cover in the monsoon and Landsat sensor gaps reduce valid observations in some years.
  • Period and boundary. The dataset ends in 2021, and results depend on the municipal boundary used.

def yearly_areas(img):
    area_km2 = ee.Image.pixelArea().divide(1e6)

    bands = ee.Image.cat([
        area_km2.multiply(img.eq(2)).rename("seasonal_km2"),
        area_km2.multiply(img.eq(3)).rename("permanent_km2"),
        area_km2.multiply(img.eq(1)).rename("notwater_km2"),
        area_km2.multiply(img.eq(0)).rename("nodata_km2"),
        area_km2.rename("total_km2"),
    ])

    sums = bands.reduceRegion(
        reducer=ee.Reducer.sum(),
        geometry=vijayawada_geom,
        scale=30,
        maxPixels=1e9,
        tileScale=4
    )

    return ee.Feature(None, sums).set("year", img.get("year"))

def ee_to_pil(rgb_image, dimensions=900):
    url = rgb_image.getThumbURL({
        "region": region_bounds,
        "dimensions": dimensions,
        "format": "png",
    })
    resp = requests.get(url, timeout=180)
    resp.raise_for_status()
    return Image.open(io.BytesIO(resp.content))
